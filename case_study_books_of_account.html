TAX COMPLIANCE · RECORD-KEEPING

The Imperative of Keeping Proper Books of Account Under the New Tax Regime: Risks, Statutory Mandates, and Safeguards for Taxpayers

Kolawole Ogunbowale, FCA Partner Tax Compliance · 7 min read
Section 01

Executive Summary

In Nigerian fiscal administration, tax liability is fundamentally governed by the principle of self-assessment backed by verifiable empirical evidence. A critical highlight of Nigeria's ongoing tax reform — anchored by the Nigeria Tax Act (NTA) and the Nigeria Tax Administration Act (NTAA) — is the stringent focus on record-keeping compliance.

Failing to maintain contemporaneous, proper accounting records exposes taxpayers to administrative penalties and leaves them vulnerable to Best of Judgment (BOJ) assessments. In the absence of verifiable records, the tax authority applies discretionary "rules of thumb," estimating income based on total bank inflows or industry averages — often resulting in inflated, punitive tax liabilities.

Section 02

The Legal Framework: Statutory Mandates Under the NTA and NTAA

The obligation to maintain comprehensive books of account is expressly built into the procedural and administrative pillars of the current tax legislation:

  • Section 31 of the Nigeria Tax Administration Act (NTAA) – Statutory Duty to Maintain Records: Every taxable entity or person — including tax-exempt entities — is required to maintain records and books of account sufficient to ascertain their true tax position.
  • Language & Translation Requirements (Section 31(3–4) NTAA): Records must be maintained in English. Where kept in another language, the taxpayer is legally required to provide a certified translation by a sworn translator at their own expense upon request.
  • 6-Year Retention Rule (Section 31(5) NTAA): Financial records, invoices, ledgers, and transaction logs must be retained for a minimum of 6 years after the relevant year of assessment.
  • E-Invoicing and Digitalization (Section 102 NTA / NTAA Framework): Digital record-keeping and electronic invoicing integrations are formally prioritized. Non-compliance or failure to present records upon official demand triggers administrative fines alongside statutory interest charges.
Section 03

The Danger of Default: "Best of Judgment" Assessments & Rule of Thumb Taxation

When a taxpayer fails to keep or submit proper books of account, the tax authority rejects incomplete filings and invokes its discretionary assessment powers:

  • Rejection of Deductible Expenses: Under standard tax principles, expenses are only deductible if they are proven to be wholly, exclusively, necessarily, and reasonably incurred for business operations. Without supporting documentation (invoices, receipts, contracts), the tax authority will disallow expense claims entirely, taxing total gross revenue rather than net profit.
  • Bank Inflow Exposure: In the absence of a clear ledger, tax auditors treat every credit entry or bank inflow as taxable turnover. Equity injections, inter-company transfers, reimbursed expenses, or non-taxable loans are routinely tagged as taxable revenue.
  • Turnover-Based Arbitrary Assessment: Tax authorities fall back on presumptive or profit-percentage models (e.g., assessing an arbitrary 20% deemed profit on total turnover), forcing the taxpayer to pay inflated corporate and personal income taxes.
  • Shift in Burden of Proof: In tax disputes, the legal burden of proof rests squarely on the taxpayer. Without proper books of account, a taxpayer cannot successfully object to or challenge an inflated arbitrary assessment before the Tax Appeal Tribunal (TAT) or federal courts.
Section 04

Comparative Practical Impact

Consider Beta Enterprises Ltd, an entity generating ₦100m in annual gross bank inflows with ₦80m in genuine operating expenses (₦20m actual net profit):

Operational & Tax Metric Scenario A: Proper Books Maintained Scenario B: No Records Kept (BOJ / Deemed Basis)
Documented Evidence Full general ledger, receipts, bank reconciliations. None (Personal/business accounts mixed, no invoices).
Recognized Turnover ₦100m ₦100m (All bank credits treated as income)
Allowed Business Expenses ₦80m (Fully substantiated) ₦0 (Disallowed for lack of proof)
Assessable Tax Base ₦20m Net Profit ₦100m Gross Turnover or Deemed Profit Base
Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Tax calculated only on net profit (₦20m). Tax charged on full ₦100m turnover or arbitrary %.
Administrative Outcome Predictable tax liability, fully defensible. Severe over-assessment, statutory penalties, audit failure.
Section 05

Strategic Business Benefits of Proper Record-Keeping

  1. Tax Optimization & Expense Protection: Ensures full deduction of valid business costs, capital allowances, and exempt income sources.
  2. Audit Readiness: Drastically reduces audit cycle times and eliminates exposure to administrative penalties under Section 102 of the NTA / Section 31 NTAA.
  3. Financial Transparency & Governance: Beyond tax compliance, clear accounts enable seamless access to institutional funding, bank loans, and investor capital.
Section 06

Presentation Recommendations & Actionable Takeaways

  • Institutionalize Digital Accounting: Transition away from manual tracking toward structured accounting software capable of real-time e-invoicing.
  • Enforce Strict Account Separation: Eliminate the practice of running business transactions through personal accounts to prevent credit entries from being mischaracterized as revenue.
  • Maintain Audit Trail Integrity: Ensure that general ledgers, bank statements, executed contracts, and vendor receipts are archived systematically for the statutory 6-year period.
