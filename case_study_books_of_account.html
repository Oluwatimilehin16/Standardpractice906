Building an audit-ready books of account framework

A client running business transactions through a mix of personal and corporate accounts was one audit away from having its entire turnover treated as taxable income. We rebuilt its record-keeping around the Nigeria Tax Administration Act before that audit arrived.

The challenge

Our client generated roughly ₦100m a year in gross bank inflows, with genuine operating expenses of around ₦80m. On paper, that's a ₦20m net profit. But invoices, receipts and reconciliations were incomplete, and personal and business accounts weren't kept separate. Under Section 31 of the Nigeria Tax Administration Act, every taxable person has to keep records sufficient to establish its true tax position — and without them, the tax authority is entitled to fall back on a Best of Judgment assessment: treating every bank credit as taxable turnover and disallowing expense claims it can't verify. For this client, that meant real exposure to being taxed on ₦100m of gross inflows rather than ₦20m of actual profit, with the burden of proof sitting entirely on the client to disprove it.

Our approach

01

Separated the accounts

Stopped business transactions running through personal accounts, so credit entries could no longer be mischaracterised as revenue.

02

Rebuilt the ledger

Reconstructed a general ledger with supporting invoices, receipts and contracts for every material expense, so deductions could be substantiated rather than assumed.

03

Set a retention schedule

Put a 6-year record retention policy in place in line with Section 31(5) of the NTAA, covering ledgers, invoices and transaction logs.

04

Digitised going forward

Moved the client onto structured accounting software capable of real-time e-invoicing, ahead of the digitalisation priorities under Section 102 of the NTA.

The numbers

Recognised turnover ₦100m either way — the difference is entirely in what happens next.
With proper books ₦80m in expenses fully substantiated and allowed — tax calculated on the real ₦20m net profit.
Without records (Best of Judgment) Expenses disallowed for lack of proof — tax charged on the full ₦100m turnover, or an arbitrary deemed-profit percentage.
Outcome secured A defensible, predictable tax liability, with audit trail integrity built to withstand a Tax Appeal Tribunal challenge if it ever comes to that.

Outcome & what it means going forward

The client now files on a defensible net-profit basis instead of an exposed gross-turnover one, and can produce a clean audit trail on demand. Beyond the immediate tax position, the same record-keeping work paid off in ways that outlast any one assessment:

Full protection of valid expense claims Faster, less disruptive audits Bank and investor-ready financials
