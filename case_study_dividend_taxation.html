Repositioning a dividend policy for the Excess Dividend Tax repeal

A manufacturing client sat on retained earnings it was afraid to distribute. Under the old Section 19 regime, paying a dividend in a loss-making year could trigger a second round of tax on profit that had already been taxed once. We walked the board through what changed under the Nigeria Tax Act, and rebuilt the dividend policy around it.

The challenge

Our client — a manufacturing entity with a healthy pool of retained earnings — hit an operational shock and closed a financial year with no taxable profit. The board still wanted to pay a dividend out of prior-year reserves, but under Section 19 of the old Companies Income Tax Act, that decision carried a real cost: any dividend paid in a year with no or insufficient total profit was re-characterised as taxable profit and taxed again at the full 30% corporate rate, even where the underlying earnings had already borne tax in the year they were made. For a business built around a holding structure, the same rule also caught dividends received from subsidiaries as Franked Investment Income, making a second tax charge almost unavoidable on any redistribution.

Our approach

01

Traced the earnings history

Built an audit trail showing the dividend was funded from profits already subjected to Companies Income Tax in a prior year, the evidence the Finance Act 2019 safe harbours required.

02

Modelled the exposure

Compared the tax outcome of the distribution under the pre-2019 rule, the Finance Act 2019 exemptions, and the incoming Nigeria Tax Act, so the board could see the cost of waiting.

03

Timed the distribution

Sequenced the dividend declaration against the Nigeria Tax Act's commencement, where the Excess Dividend Tax mechanism is repealed outright.

04

Reset the compliance posture

Briefed the client on the redrawn Section 19, which now governs Withholding Tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders as a final tax, and on Section 7 of the NTA for dividend classification going forward.

The numbers

Unamended CITA (pre-2019) Full ₦200m dividend re-taxed as profit — ₦60m in Companies Income Tax owed on earnings already taxed once.
Finance Act 2019 era Exempt, but only with a proven audit trail of prior Companies Income Tax payment — no margin for incomplete records.
Current NTA position Exempt outright. The Excess Dividend Tax mechanism no longer exists — ₦0 in additional Companies Income Tax on the distribution.
Withholding tax 10% at source throughout — now the final tax for non-resident shareholders under the Nigeria Tax Administration Act, with no further liability or refund claim to manage.

Outcome & what it means going forward

The client distributed the dividend without triggering a secondary tax charge, and now runs its holding structure without the constant reconciliation the old Section 19 demanded. More broadly, the repeal changes how we advise clients with similar structures:

Investor confidence in reinvestment Simplified holding company structuring Compliance focus shifts to WHT remittance
